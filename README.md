## Research Premise

Different organizations can receive similar demand signals and still make substantially different inventory decisions.

This can be represented conceptually as:

[
InventoryDecision_{j,t}
=======================

f_j(
Demand,
Forecast,
Information,
HumanFactors,
Coordination,
Organization,
SupplyConditions,
Capabilities,
Risk
)
]

where:

[
f_{CompanyA} \neq f_{CompanyB}
]

because organizations differ in their:

* information systems,
* decision processes,
* human experience,
* incentives,
* organizational structure,
* coordination mechanisms,
* capabilities,
* financial constraints,
* risk tolerance,
* suppliers,
* markets,
* and strategic priorities.

A particularly important research question is therefore:

> **Why do organizations facing similar information make different supply chain decisions, and which of those differences generate or prevent logistics waste?**

## Coordination & Organizational Fragmentation

A specific area of interest is the relationship between **organizational fragmentation, cross-functional coordination, and supply chain decision quality**.

Functional specialization is necessary, but fragmented decision-making can increase risk when departments operate with different information, incentives, objectives, or interpretations of demand.

A working hypothesis is:

```text
High Organizational Fragmentation
                +
Low Cross-Functional Coordination
                ↓
Information Loss / Delay
                ↓
Conflicting Objectives
                ↓
Local Optimization
                ↓
Decision Distortion
                ↓
Supply Chain Risk
                ↓
Inventory & Operational Waste
```

Coordination may therefore act as a **moderating capability** that reduces the negative effects of organizational fragmentation.

## Human Factors in Supply Chain Decisions

Supply chain decisions are not purely mathematical.

They may also be influenced by:

* fear of stockouts,
* overconfidence,
* optimism bias,
* recency bias,
* loss aversion,
* managerial pressure,
* previous disruptions,
* experience,
* intuition,
* organizational culture,
* and individual risk perception.

This creates an important research intersection between:

**Operations Research + Artificial Intelligence + Behavioral Operations + Organizational Decision-Making**

## Research Questions

Some of the questions guiding my current research direction are:

1. How are inventory decisions actually formed inside organizations?
2. How do forecasting uncertainty and human judgment interact in inventory decision-making?
3. How does organizational fragmentation affect decision quality?
4. Can cross-functional coordination reduce supply chain decision risk?
5. Why do organizations facing similar demand signals make different inventory decisions?
6. Which decision factors generate excess inventory, stockouts, obsolescence, and logistics waste?
7. How can the economic and environmental impact of poor supply chain decisions be quantified?
8. Can AI identify behavioral or organizational patterns associated with poor inventory decisions?
9. Can AI-enabled decision systems improve decisions without eliminating valuable human knowledge?
10. How can inventory optimization incorporate economic, service, risk, waste, and environmental objectives simultaneously?
